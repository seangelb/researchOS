# External evidence for a Carvana daily sales approximation

Research checked on **2026-09-11**. This note separates public vendor disclosures, the supplied aggregate workbook, and our reconstruction hypotheses. No vendor VIN-level observations or transaction labels were acquired. No vendor was contacted, no account was created, and no purchase was made. This external-source research used zero Carvana detail-page visits. The local experiment and its numerical results are documented separately in the research notebook and log.

The practical choice is a transparent tracked-population proxy using native status transitions and comparable inventory absences. Independent title/registration evidence is the strongest next validation input if a suitable licensed sample can be obtained. MarketCheck provides useful methodological comparison and broader listing histories, but agreement with it would principally test another listing-inference system.

## The official target and its limits

Carvana defines retail units as customer sales, **including retail Marketplace partner vehicles and net of seven-day returns**. Retail revenue recognition occurs at delivery/pickup, with an expected-return reserve. Its website-inventory metric includes available, purchase-in-progress/reserved, and reservable unfinished vehicles. The Q2 2026 filing reports **197,325 retail units** and **77,971 website units** at quarter-end. These are different flow and stock measures. [Carvana Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A key metrics and results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1690820/000169082026000055/cvna-20260630.htm).

For scale only, `197325 / 91 = 2168.4066` units per calendar day during Q2. This is a quarterly arithmetic average, not a current daily observation or a calibration target for the selected cohort. Neither the filing nor the reviewed vendor documentation establishes when Carvana's native `Sold` label changes relative to delivery or returns. Consequently, status events are not a demonstrated lower bound on the official net-sales metric. A Marketplace vehicle credited to its partner in a registry or listing-attribution system may still belong in Carvana's reported retail units; reconcile that attribution before comparing totals.

## Comparison and recommended role

| Approach | Evidence and assumption | Advantage | Main failure modes and cost | Role in this study |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First native non-Sold to exact Sold | One status transition per retailer/VIN episode; count only after usable prior observation | Closest directly observable native sale signal | Initially Sold and repeated checks add no new transition; timing is interval-censored; returns and contradictory states; browser checks consume the detail budget | Implement as a status-based proxy and expose native counts |
| Three-/seven-day persistent absence | Complete, comparable query collections; elapsed absence threshold and no intervening reappearance | Broad coverage through existing inventory collection | Ranking, pagination, changed filters, missing days, relisting, wholesale transfer; longer threshold delays availability | Compare both thresholds; never substitute a missing collection for observed absence |
| Combined rule | Deduplicated union of native transitions and qualified absences; pending history provides an explicitly chosen sensitivity | Uses complementary signals without invented conversion rates | Overlap double counting; status/absence share a website; narrow versus broad scenarios are not statistical intervals | Preferred simple architecture if empirical coverage supports both components |
| Inventory flow and stratified sampling | End = beginning + additions - disappearances for the same population; future weighted estimates require known inclusion probabilities/stratum counts | Detects accounting and coverage errors; potentially scalable | Identity changes and query changes mimic movement; turnover differs by model, age, price and partner type | Use flow as reconciliation; no national scaling of the 33 selected VINs |
| Cross-dealer VIN histories / MarketCheck | Crawl-derived history and inferred sales | Detects another listing ID, another dealer, or reappearance outside a narrow query | Market-wide crawl gaps and attribution errors; paid access/retention rights needed | External listing benchmark, not independent completed-sale labels |
| Registration, title and ownership evidence | Provider-classified ownership events, with source, event date, publication date and seller attribution | Different evidence lineage from disappearing listings | Renewals/corrected titles, dealer ownership, reporting delay, state restrictions and retrospective revisions; licensed access | Best candidate for independent corroboration after event-level review |
| SEC/company reporting | Reported aggregate net units | Official company-wide quarterly denominator | No individual VIN/day outcomes; selected inventory population differs | Aggregate reference once time, coverage and partner attribution match |

## What the vendors actually document

**MarketCheck.** Its inferred-sale rule requires a VIN absent from the latest active crawl, a latest `status_date` across that VIN's listings older than seven days, and seller attribution to the previously searchable listing. That is a cross-domain absence algorithm, not a dealer-reported transaction. The limited local model queries cannot recreate its coverage merely by using seven days. [Methodology](https://docs.marketcheck.com/docs/guides/data/cars/inventory/key-concepts).

Past Inventory Search covers recent expired/inferred-sold US/Canadian listings, publishes daily by 11:00 UTC, and offers a 90-day window. A seven-day classification delay is distinct from an exact transaction date. Its price filter is an advertised-price field, so retain asking-price terminology. [Past Inventory Search](https://docs.marketcheck.com/docs/api/cars/inventory/past-inventory-search). The VIN History API exposes source, seller, listing identity and first/last observation dates, allowing cross-dealer review without merging retailer histories. A zero-result response is not evidence that a VIN never traded. [VIN History](https://docs.marketcheck.com/docs/api/cars/vehicle-history/history-by-vin).

The current standard API terms prohibit independent persistent datasets and systematic extraction into a standalone database; a separate enterprise agreement may be needed. Obtain explicit permission covering raw snapshot retention, SQLite, backtests and research outputs before integrating vendor data. Public API documentation is accessible; no data access or quotation was tested here. [API terms, sections 4-6](https://www.marketcheck.com/terms_of_service/).

**Experian AutoCount.** The current product page offers new/used sales reporting by dealer and geography, with client access and a contact process; it does not state a current state-by-state lag or complete dealer-identity coverage. [Current automotive market reporting](https://www.experian.com/automotive/autocount). Its **2012** dealer brochure describes monthly DMV analysis, nationwide vehicle activity, dealer attribution in 34 states, and availability two to three weeks after month-end. These are historical specifications, **not a current service guarantee**. [Dated dealer brochure](https://stg1.experian.com/assets/automotive/brochures/autocount-dealer-report-ss.pdf). CFPB's 2021 analysis of 2015 AutoCount data confirms monthly deidentified title/registration inputs and shows that customers can receive aggregated rather than individual records. [CFPB data description, section 3.1](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_subprime-auto_data-point_2021-09.pdf).

For a VIN-level trial, Experian's Auto AccuSelect is a separate relevant product: documented fields include current-owner start date, title date/state, duplicate/corrected-title flags, and registration date/state. Their existence does not establish exact retail-sale timing or automatic Carvana seller identification. [Auto AccuSelect](https://www.experian.com/automotive/auto-accuselect). Current price, permitted investment-research use, VIN delivery and archival rights remain unverified.

**Mobility Global.** Its market-reporting page documents monthly refresh, US/DC and Canadian used-vehicle coverage, historical data and geographic detail. Its general used-vehicle methodology uses proprietary algorithms to determine ownership changes. It links Market Monitor for selling-dealer insights and AutoCreditInsight for monthly counts with loan data; both linked detail pages returned 404 during this review. The accessible documentation does not settle Carvana entity coverage, event-level export, state reporting lag or archival licensing. A daily-updated underlying repository must not be confused with daily final transaction reporting. [Market-reporting product](https://www.mobilityglobal.com/en-us/products/automotive-market-data-analysis). Registration-derived records could independently corroborate listing signals, but provider ownership classifications still require validation.

**Vehicle-history reports / NMVTIS.** NMVTIS consumer reports emphasize title state/date, title brands, odometer, total-loss and salvage information, not an exhaustive seller-attributed retail-sale ledger. [DOJ report definition](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/nmvtis_understandingvhr). Access is through approved report providers, with their own delivery and use terms. [DOJ provider directory](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/nmvtis_vehiclehistory). A new title date alone cannot identify a Carvana retail sale. The retained local report experiments should be reviewed directly; historical narrative counts are not refreshed empirical measurements in this external-source note.

## Smallest useful independent validation experiment

After the next comparable observations, freeze a dated set of candidate transitions/absences **and noncandidate controls** across tracked models and partner types. Request only VIN, source category, event type/date precision, seller entity/confidence, and publication/revision clocks. Obtain two dated deliveries after the provider's stated reporting window to expose late arrivals. This is proposed continuation, not authorization to purchase or contact anyone.

Keep three outcome classes: corroborated retail transfer, observed competing event/reappearance, and unresolved. No record is not a negative label. Evaluate later evidence against frozen predictions and retain first-available clocks; never leak future reports into an earlier estimate. Report resolution rate, listing reappearance and status persistence first. Estimate transaction precision only for independently resolved candidate outcomes, with unresolved cases visible. Recall needs a suitable independently identified sales denominator including sales the proxy missed. Until then, a seven-day absence sensitivity is an assumption-based proxy, not a calibrated probability.

### Workbook arithmetic and population audit

The audited file is `C:\Users\Sean\Downloads\CVNA_Q3_2026_Summary_Free (1).xlsx`, SHA-256 `d9c7caa593793126c0f79098545e4ca0881746dfe1c4e950ab85ea9fe37cbed5`, unchanged before/after. This is the **40-day** vintage through August 9, not the earlier 39-day file ending August 8. [Reproduction script](../scripts/audit_carvana_vendor_metrics.py) and [final audit JSON](../data/experiments/vendor_method_audit/20260911T162442Z/audit.json) retain source cells, formulas, comments, date coverage and all daily calculations. The script reads the existing workbook reader; it neither saves nor recalculates the workbook in Excel. The JSON lets Notebook 23 show the arithmetic with pandas without requiring the Downloads file.

- **Run rate is a prior-year seasonal-shape projection.** `I41=SUM(C$2:C41)/SUM(D$2:D41)-1`; `J41=(I41+1)*SUM(D$2:D$93)`. Through August 9, current sales total **91,901**, prior-year matching dates **65,581.14985868326**, and full prior quarter **155,941**. Therefore J41 reconstructs to **218,525.1993**, from QTD growth **40.1333%**. Flat calendar extrapolation gives `91901/40*92 = 211,372.3`. They differ by **7,152.8993** units. The formula assumes the remaining quarter follows last year's relative daily shape; it is not a trailing-seven-day average. A uniform rescaling of all prior-year days cancels in this ratio.
- **Fractional prior-year counts are exactly consistent with aggregate normalization.** Every D2:D93 value equals an integer times **155941/158863 = 0.9816067932747083**, with maximum numerical reconstruction error **4.55e-13**. The reconstructed integers start 1,589, 1,756, 1,723, 1,736 and sum to **158,863**. D96 explicitly contains 155,941, the official Q3 2025 retail count. D95 sums the already-normalized column and D97 divides D96 by D95, now approximately one; D99 onward repeat a rescaling formula. Thus a single proportional adjustment explains all fractions. The origin and first-available vintage of the inferred integers are not established, and those integers must not be called raw sales. In particular, normalized historical daily totals matching an official quarter are not evidence of accurate daily timing. [Official Q3 2025 result](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1690820/000169082025000352/ex99_2q32025.htm).
- **The stock-flow residual is real and unexplained.** July 2: `BL2 + S3 - BL3 = 74347 + 3941 - 75792 = 2496`, versus C3=2,376 sales, leaving **120**. Over July 2–August 9 the same algebra implies **101,913 exits** against **89,742 sales**, residual **12,171**: 38 positive intervals, one negative, no exact matches. These identities are conditional on S being additions to BL's population; that assumption is not disclosed. Relists, different cuts/populations, non-sale removals and adjusted sales all remain possible. Do not name the residual cancellations.
- **Timing contamination is directly suggested by the cells.** July 6 repeats July 5 inventory **72,229** and pending **15,194**, reports **zero listings**, but has **2,152 sales**. Its residual is −2,152; July 7's is +2,613. A stale inventory snapshot or delayed collection can generate such offsetting daily errors. A22 notes late collection and adjusted sales with unadjusted orders/listings, but mentions January dates on a July row. A35 notes a run started roughly 2.5 hours late. Neither comment identifies the July 6 failure. Retain that distinction rather than assigning its cause.
- **Pending stock does not reconcile directly to sales.** If July 2's O3=3,291 orders were pending entries, the pending-stock change from 16,382 to 16,334 implies **3,339 pending exits**, exceeding sales by **963**. Those exits could include several statuses, and O could count repeated attempts. The conditional identity is useful precisely because it exposes the missing definition.
- **Some metric populations are incompatible.** AK2's sixteen rounded model-year shares fit a smallest integer denominator of **111** within a search through 500; all sixteen are incompatible with BL2=74,347 at the stated six-decimal precision. Multiples of 111 also fit, so 111 is not a proven sample size. CE2 reports two equal sold-year shares, while C2=2,159 is odd; they cannot be exact shares of the same unweighted full sale count. BJ2 lists only year 2019 despite S2=3,846 listings. A filtered subset, lost scope or erroneous output could explain these patterns. The workbook does not identify which; do not use these mix columns to weight all reported sales or inventory.
- **Coverage and calculation defects remain visible.** C/O/S/BK/BL/B/W have 40 numeric dates; AL has 39, including a missing sold-price observation. D has 92 prior-year dates. T continues for **69 dates through September 7**, which does not extend daily C coverage. All **829 formulas** lack cached results. There are 120 `Null`, 15 `nan` and five `nan%` text tokens. P agrees with rolling seven-day O for all 34 fully observed windows; T differs from rolling S in seven windows, July 7–13. R7 is `-99.72%`, whereas O7/Q7−1 is **−11.4277%**. F2 includes an unsupported in-quarter carry-in of 13,225. These are source properties, not repairs made by this audit.

**“Sold Price” is not established as a transaction price.** AL2 is a supplied text value `27703.99` with no formula, VIN population or realized-price lineage. The accompanying APR, term, down-payment and monthly-payment columns could be averages of advertised offers. JXCE explicitly describes the **list price of sales** as one input to an ASP projection, supplemented by Marketplace share, delivery fees, Marketplace GPU and attachment rates; it notes uncertainty in the latter inputs. That disclosure strengthens an asking-price interpretation for JXCE's underlying vehicle series but does not define Clarity's AL column. Keep AL labeled “vendor Sold Price; basis unverified,” and local data labeled asking price. [JXCE's February 18 explanation](https://www.jxcedata.com/post/carvana-q4-another-precisely-projected-quarter).

### Cross-source corroboration: what it can and cannot settle

MarketCheck's VIN histories can expose a second dealer, a changed listing and a later reappearance. Its default attribution chooses one searchable seller using location/name/address/domain evidence; a Marketplace partner can therefore receive attribution even if Carvana includes the unit in its reported retail sales. Its sale classifier uses cross-domain disappearance and a seven-day threshold, so agreement corroborates a listing inference, not a completed transaction. [Attribution and inferred-sale rules](https://docs.marketcheck.com/docs/guides/data/cars/inventory/key-concepts), [VIN history fields](https://docs.marketcheck.com/docs/api/cars/vehicle-history/history-by-vin).

Experian currently documents VIN ownership start, title/registration dates and corrected/duplicate-title flags, but not a public current lag guarantee or complete Carvana seller coverage. Its old AutoCount brochure's two-to-three-weeks-after-month-end timing and 34-state dealer attribution are **2012 specifications**, not today's contract. [Auto AccuSelect fields](https://www.experian.com/automotive/auto-accuselect), [dated brochure](https://stg1.experian.com/assets/automotive/brochures/autocount-dealer-report-ss.pdf). NMVTIS's specified core fields concern title, brands, odometer, total loss and salvage; title date alone supplies neither a Carvana seller nor economic sale date. [DOJ definition](https://vehiclehistory.bja.ojp.gov/nmvtis_understandingvhr).

The next independent sample must distinguish retail buyer transfer from dealer transfer, renewal, corrected title and interstate move; retain event, receipt and first-publication dates and revisions; and reconcile Marketplace seller attribution. Review candidate VINs and random noncandidate controls after the provider's measured reporting lag, then obtain a later vintage to quantify backfill. Missing records remain unresolved. No licensed sample, vendor contact or purchase occurred here.
